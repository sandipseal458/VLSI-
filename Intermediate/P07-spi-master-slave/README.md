# P07 — SPI Master and Slave (Full-Duplex Byte Exchange)

This project is a complete SPI link in Verilog: a master that generates the
serial clock and a slave that answers it, exchanging one byte in each
direction during the same frame. I wrote it to understand what actually
happens on the four SPI wires (`SCLK`, `MOSI`, `MISO`, `CS`) instead of
just reading about it, and to get comfortable with clock-domain reasoning —
the master is synchronous to `clk`, the slave is synchronous to `SCLK`, and
they only meet on the wires in between.

---

## The Interface

| Signal | Width | Driven by | Meaning |
|--------|-------|-----------|---------|
| `clk` | 1 | system | Master's system clock (100 MHz in simulation) |
| `load` | 1 | testbench | Pulse to start a new 8-bit frame |
| `tx_data` | 8 | master input | Byte the master sends out on MOSI |
| `rx_data` | 8 | master output | Byte captured from MISO |
| `sclk` | 1 | master | Serial clock, 25 MHz (clk ÷ 4) |
| `MOSI` | 1 | master | Master Out, Slave In |
| `MISO` | 1 | slave | Master In, Slave Out |
| `CS` | 1 | master | Chip select, active low, one per frame |

The slave side takes `tx_data` (what it wants to send), returns `rx_data`
(what it captured from MOSI), and listens to `sclk`, `MOSI`, `MISO`, `CS`.

---

## How I Designed It

**The clock.** The master divides `clk` with a 2-bit counter: `sclk = count[1]`
gives a 40 ns period, i.e. 25 MHz from a 100 MHz clock. I chose to derive
`sclk` from `clk` rather than build a separate clock, because two unrelated
clocks in one design are the classic source of timing-closure pain later.
Everything in the master stays on `posedge clk`; `sclk` is just a data
signal that happens to toggle in a fixed rhythm.

**Mode 0 timing.** The master samples `MISO` on the rising edge of `sclk`
and updates `MOSI` on the falling edge. That is SPI Mode 0
(CPOL = 0, CPHA = 0): data is stable around the sampling edge, so neither
side races its own bit. The slave mirrors this exactly — it samples `MOSI`
on `posedge sclk` and drives `MISO` on `negedge sclk`.

```text
clk     _|·|_|·|_|·|_|·|_|·|_|·|_|·|_|·| ...
sclk    ____········____········____·······   (one bit per high phase)
MOSI    ----<b7>----<b6>----<b5>---- ...     (changes on sclk falling)
MISO    ----<b7>----<b6>----<b5>---- ...     (sampled on sclk rising)
CS      ¯¯¯¯¯¯¯¯¯|________________|¯¯¯¯¯¯¯¯   (low for exactly one byte)
```

**Why MSB first.** SPI has no fixed bit order in the standard, but MSB-first
is what nearly every memory and sensor expects, so I made both sides agree
on it and documented it rather than leaving it implicit.

**Chip select as the frame boundary.** `load` latches `tx_data`, pulls `CS`
low, and sets an `active` flag. After eight sampled bits the master latches
`rx_data`, releases `CS`, and stops `sclk` at a known level. The slave uses
the falling edge of `CS` as its "reload" event — that is the only thing that
loads its own `tx_data` and resets its bit counter, so a second frame works
without any handshake between the two modules.

**Full duplex.** Because both sides shift on the same edges, the master's
byte travels out on `MOSI` while the slave's byte travels back on `MISO`.
One 8-bit frame, two bytes moved. That symmetry is the part I found most
satisfying once it worked on the waveform.

## Verification

`tb_spi.v` is self-checking. It runs three exchanges and verifies **both**
directions every time, plus the chip-select behaviour:

| Test | Master sends | Slave sends | Checks |
|------|--------------|-------------|--------|
| T1 | `0xA5` | `0x3C` | master received `0x3C`, slave received `0xA5`, CS low during frame, CS high after |
| T2 | `0x3C` | `0xA5` | values swapped, same four checks |
| T3 | `0x00` | `0xFF` | boundary bytes (all-zeros / all-ones patterns) |

12 checks total, each printed as `PASS`/`FAIL`, with a final
`RESULT: PASSED` line. The testbench also dumps `tb_spi.vcd` so I can open
the frame in a waveform viewer and count the eight `sclk` pulses myself.

Simulation log: [`simulation_output.txt`](simulation_output.txt)

## What I Would Improve

Two things bother me if I were handing this to a team:

1. **The slave has no clock-domain synchronizer.** Its flops run on
   `posedge sclk`, which is generated by the master, so in simulation the
   alignment is perfect by construction. On a real FPGA I would add a
   synchroniser on `CS` and treat `sclk` as a data-capture clock, or run the
   slave logic in the system domain and edge-detect `sclk`.
2. **No CPOL/CPHA configurability.** Real SPI peripherals support four
   modes. I deliberately implemented one mode well instead of four modes
   badly, but the next step is a `mode` input that flips sampling and
   shifting edges.

## Interview Notes

- *Why does the slave reload on the falling edge of CS?* Because CS is the
  only signal that is unambiguously "a new frame is starting" — there is no
  byte-length signalling on `MOSI`.
- *What would you check first if the bytes come back shifted by one bit?*
  A phase mismatch between sampling and shifting edges — i.e. the design is
  in a different SPI mode than the peer, or one side updates on the same
  edge it samples on.
- *Where is the clock boundary?* Master logic on `posedge clk`, slave logic
  on `posedge sclk`; the wires are the crossing point.
